# Move catalogue

The source is 27 videos by Mahesh Shenoy (FloatHeadPhysics), analysed on 2026-10-08. "n/27" is the number of videos that use the move. A "≥" count is a lower bound: the move was named independently by different analysts and merged afterwards. All examples are paraphrased.

## 1. Opening

| Move | n/27 | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Real confusion | 16 | Start from something that genuinely did not make sense, often to the narrator himself: "light has no mass, so why does gravity bend it?" |
| Reject the label | 11 | Treat the textbook answer as a restatement. "Iron is stable because of high binding energy per nucleon" names the fact; the real question is why the stability curve rises, peaks and falls. |
| Stakes up front, paid off at the end | ≥2 (also seen in held-out tests) | Before the nucleus puzzle he shows what depends on it: stars stop fusing at iron, radioactivity, Earth's hot core. |
| Rediscover, don't recall | ≥5 | "Pretend we know nothing about orbitals" and rebuild the shapes from standing waves. Official names (quantum numbers, tensor) arrive after the idea. |

## 2. Break the naive model

| Move | n/27 | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Naive model, then break it | 21 | "More neutrons are more glue, so more neutrons means more stable." A counterexample breaks it: a helium nucleus with 14 neutrons should be very stable but does not exist. |
| Chain of models | (within 21) | Photoelectric effect: brightness should set electron speed → Lenard's trigger idea → light in chunks. Each step is killed by a new finding. |
| Staged dialogue | ≥17 | He voices the wrong answer. A mentor (Feynman, Einstein) asks one simple question and withholds the answer until he finds it. |
| Voice the objection | 19 | "But if every patch of Earth accelerates outward, why doesn't Earth expand?" He states the objection before the learner can. |

## 4. Engines that produce the idea

| Move | n/27 | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Tinker one variable | 18 | Double the speed: the velocity change doubles *and* the time halves, so acceleration quadruples. Then triple it to confirm the square. |
| Demand the mechanism | ≥13 | "Mass goes to infinity" is not a cause. Zoom in: force signals between the ship's atoms take ever longer paths. A proof without a mechanism or a plug-in derivation does not count. |
| Reframe | ≥12 | Study an easier twin (two clocks before a cluster), move the problem to geometry, strip to the core, step back when stuck. |
| Hold the invariant | ≥10 | Keep "uniform motion is undetectable" fixed, then ask what must give. Answer: time and length. |
| Define the word / count | ≥10 | What would "spin" have to mean? Particles circling a centre, and an electron has no parts. "How many numbers do we need?" leads to a tensor. |
| Spot the coincidence | ≥9 | Why does gravitational mass always equal inertial mass? A good model makes the match necessary, not lucky. |

## 6. Attack the result

| Move | n/27 | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Limits and special cases | 18 | At low speed the result must reduce to Newton. Zero cross terms give back Pythagoras. |
| Two ledgers | (within ≥18) | Compute the atom's energy in two frames. 8 ≠ 7.8, so the mass must have dropped. |
| Decisive test | (within ≥18) | Which experiment would only one model pass? Options are eliminated one by one. |
| Try to break it | (within ≥18) | Attack the second law four ways. Each time, count the whole system: the hidden part restores the law. |
| Lucky-alignment check | (within ≥18) | A model passes a test only because the axes happened to align. Rotate them and it fails. |

## Anti-patterns (what the method rejects)
- A label or restatement presented as a reason.
- A plug-in derivation with no picture.
- An analogy that smuggles in the thing it explains.
- The right answer for the wrong cause.
- A technology limit dressed up as a principle.
- A memorised tool where re-deriving it would carry the insight.
